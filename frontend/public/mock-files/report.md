# Summary

In this dataset of 44,649 blood cells from hospitalized COVID‑19 patients and healthy donors, we found that CD14 monocytes exhibit a significantly stronger interferon‑alpha response and markedly lower HLA‑class II expression in COVID‑19 patients compared to healthy donors. The ISG signature in CD16 monocytes did not reach statistical significance.

## Approved findings

### Interferon-alpha response in CD14 monocytes is elevated in COVID‑19
The Hallmark interferon-alpha response score is higher in CD14 monocytes from COVID‑19 patients (mean difference = 0.1937, Cohen’s d = 1.721, 7 COVID‑19 vs 6 healthy donors), indicating a robust type‑I interferon signature in these innate cells during active infection. This result **reproduces** the published observation of an interferon‑stimulated gene signature in monocytes from COVID‑19 patients.

### HLA‑class II expression is downregulated in CD14 monocytes in COVID‑19
HLA‑class II mean expression is lower in CD14 monocytes from COVID‑19 patients than healthy donors (mean difference = -0.8772, Cohen’s d = -3.369, 7 COVID‑19 vs 6 healthy donors), reflecting strong suppression of antigen‑presentation capacity in these cells during severe infection.

## Rejected hypotheses

- **ISG signature in CD16 Monocytes** (p_adj = 0.0739): The donor‑level comparison of ISG scores between COVID‑19 patients and healthy donors did not achieve the 0.05 significance threshold, and the result was rejected due to insufficient adjusted p‑value.
